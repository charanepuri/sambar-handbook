📜 History of Sambar

Sambar is a lentil-based vegetable stew that is central to South Indian cuisine. Its history is a fascinating blend of legend and historical records.

According to popular tradition, Sambar originated in the 17th century during the reign of the Maratha rulers of Thanjavur.

The Legend of Sambhaji

The most famous story says that Shahaji I, the Maratha ruler of Thanjavur, attempted to prepare the Maharashtrian dish Amti.

Since kokum was unavailable, tamarind was used instead. Toor dal replaced moong dal, and vegetables were added to the preparation.

Prince Sambhaji loved the dish, and it was subsequently named Sambar in his honor.

Earlier Historical References

  • 1517: The Telugu poem Amuktamalyada mentions a preparation called sambarampuchintapandu.
  • 17th Century: Food historian K.T. Achaya documented references to huli, a sour lentil dish from Karnataka.
  • Sangam Age: Ancient Tamil dishes such as Mohana Kalavai may have been early predecessors of modern Sambar.

Evolution of Sambar

Over centuries, Sambar evolved by incorporating local ingredients such as tamarind, lentils, vegetables, and regional spice blends.

Today, there are more than 50 varieties of Sambar across South India. Every state has its own unique preparation and flavor profile.

Sambar Today

Sambar remains one of the most beloved dishes in India and is commonly served with:

  • 🍚 Rice
  • 🥞 Dosa
  • 🥟 Idli
  • 🍩 Medu Vada

More than just a dish, Sambar represents South Indian heritage, hospitality, and culinary tradition.
